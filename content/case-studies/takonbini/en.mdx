# Takonbini

## Summary

When I lived in Japan, I wanted to know what was new that week at the Lawson near
my home. I had enjoyed konbini food since moving there: it tasted good, was
inexpensive, and gave me something different to try. I built Takonbini to answer
that question for myself.

Takonbini brings products from Lawson, 7-Eleven and FamilyMart into one catalog,
with English, Japanese and Traditional Chinese interfaces. Visitors can search,
filter by store, category, region or price, and open a product without losing
their browsing context. I hope it can also help konbini fans outside Japan discover
what they would like to try.

This is my personal project. The engineering story centers on preserving that
browsing context as the catalog grows. The implementation keeps a bounded window
of product data, renders the rows near the viewport, and restores a product anchor
when a visitor returns. The first results are rendered on the server; a separate
Browse route provides ordinary links through the catalog.

The evidence includes a controlled long-browsing fixture and a paired CSS-delivery
experiment. Their measurements have explicit limits. Fresh production checks
confirm a populated catalog and product-dialog focus return, while an automated
catalog accessibility finding remains open. Retailer listings do not guarantee
stock at a particular shop. Reliability and final release acceptance also remain
unfinished.

## Keeping a place without keeping every product

The original catalog appended fetched pages and kept their cards mounted. That
made returning to an earlier product straightforward, but retained more data and
DOM as browsing continued. Rendering fewer cards alone would leave the query
cache free to grow.

The chosen design separates a compact directory of visited pages from their
resident product payloads. It retains at most five pages of 20 catalog products
and uses measured rows to virtualize the visible cards. An evicted page can be
fetched using its recorded cursor rather than replaying every earlier request.
The originating product remains the reference for restoring position and focus.

Simply capping the existing forward-only infinite query would discard the earlier
payloads without providing a reliable way back. The dedicated window coordinator
adds that recovery path. The tradeoff is more restoration logic and occasional
requests when a visitor revisits evicted data. Changed or missing products need a
bounded fallback; this is not a frozen historical view of the catalog.

Product details open in a native dialog from the catalog and also have direct
URLs. Dismissal returns focus to the originating card. A history-navigation bug
showed why timing matters: the browser ignored a focus attempt while the catalog
was still inert. Restoration now belongs to the catalog owner, which waits until
the results can receive focus.

## Making the first response useful

The homepage renders its first product results on the server. A separate blocking
stylesheet request still delayed the first paint in the controlled rendering
experiment. The chosen implementation uses the framework's inline-CSS option to
include the complete generated stylesheet in the first response.

This adds bytes to the HTML. It also uses an experimental framework option, so
compiled-output checks verify that the full stylesheet is present and that no
blocking stylesheet link has returned. The implementation avoids maintaining a
second hand-written critical-CSS subset.

The same investigation reproduced a server-rendering defect: an immediately
collectable query could lose its prefetched products before the lazy route read
them. The fix retains that seed for the request-scoped server query client.
Browser cache eviction remains separate, preserving the bounded browsing policy.

## Verified outcomes

### Bounded browsing in a controlled fixture

A recorded 25 October 2026 fixture traversed more than 50 pages and encountered up
to 1,100 synthetic products. Across 96 samples, at most 100 catalog products were
resident and 21 product links were mounted. A deep refresh restored the selected
product within 0.125 CSS pixels using two client page reads plus the server seed.

Those are fixture observations, not a fixed total-browser-memory guarantee. Image
caches, browser internals and different devices are outside that bound. The
[measurement report](https://github.com/seancheong/takonbini-web/blob/6d9e63e78fd8cd77449214773dbbd65fcb3f8889/docs/evidence/catalog-memory-2026-10-25.md)
records the method, real-content checks and remaining acceptance work.

### A controlled CSS-delivery comparison

Two baseline runs measured LCP at 1.505 and 1.506 seconds. Two candidate runs
measured 0.705 and 0.706 seconds, with CLS 0 throughout. Compressed HTML increased
by 9,958 bytes. The experiment used the same frozen public products and assets,
a local mirror, an emulated mobile viewport, CPU slowdown and Slow 4G.

This isolates browser delivery. It excludes API and platform latency and does not
establish a production speedup, field INP result or worldwide performance
improvement. The [rendering report](https://github.com/seancheong/takonbini-web/blob/6d9e63e78fd8cd77449214773dbbd65fcb3f8889/docs/evidence/homepage-rendering-2026-10-26.md)
records the individual samples and the HTML-size tradeoff.

### Current production checks

On 1 November 2026, fresh checks found real catalog products in the English,
Japanese and Traditional Chinese interfaces. An English product dialog loaded its
actual description, price, release date and region. Escape dismissed it and
returned keyboard focus to its product link. The 390px catalog check had no
horizontal overflow. These are scoped observations, not a full device matrix.

Fresh axe scans found no violations in the loaded product dialog or mobile filter
panel. The English catalog scan reported a visible-label/accessibility-name
mismatch on 12 mounted links. That finding remains open. Lighthouse's initial-page
Accessibility score of 100 does not override it.

### Fresh production lab samples

Three sequential mobile Lighthouse runs on 1 November measured LCP at 2.571,
1.400 and 1.387 seconds, with Performance scores of 92, 100 and 100. Desktop LCP
was 0.693, 0.568 and 0.579 seconds. CLS was 0 in all six samples. One mobile
sample exceeded 2.5 seconds, despite the lower median.

These used Lighthouse 13.5.0's simulated mobile and desktop profiles against the
live site. There was no matched baseline, forced cold state or field data. They
cannot establish a before-and-after improvement or replace the product's adopted
release-acceptance method.

## Reflection

The useful unit of restoration is the product a visitor was reading, together
with its position in the viewport. A pixel offset alone cannot account for an
evicted page, a different column count or translated text wrapping differently.
That is the design lesson I would carry into another long-browsing interface.

The rendering investigation also changed the diagnosis. The measured LCP element
was a heading, so reducing product-image bytes would not have addressed the
observed blocking stylesheet request. The comparison tested that mechanism under
controlled conditions before treating it as a production claim.

## Remaining work

The catalog-link accessible-name finding needs resolution and a fresh check.
Broader device and assistive-technology coverage, field interaction performance,
operational recovery and alerting evidence, actual cost observation and the final
stability window remain open in the product tracker. This case study does not
claim those gates have passed.

The product reads retailer listings. Prices and availability may vary by location,
and translated descriptions have not received a comprehensive editorial review.
There are no defensible adoption, conversion or business-impact measurements in
this case study. Reaching konbini fans around the world is an ambition, not a
measured outcome.
